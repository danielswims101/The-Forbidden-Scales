The Forbidden
Scales

An epic dragon saga of loyalty, ancient power, and the bonds forged between unlikely companions — written by five voices, one world.

In a world where dragon bloodlines are destiny and elemental powers define every hierarchy, five extraordinary dragons and their companions are about to discover that the greatest power is not the one born into you — it is the one you choose to become. The scales tipping. The balance fraying. Something forbidden is waking up.

Dragon Records

Five dragons. Four authors. Five corners of the same world. Each character is written by a different member of the team, giving every dragon a wholly distinct voice — which is exactly what makes the series work. These are not side characters. Every single one is a main character in their own right.

A
Agate
WindScale  ·  Queen's Regent  ·  Written by Elina
WindScale Pescatarian Photographic Memory Queen's Regent
Appearance
Agate is immediately striking — her scales are a layered blend of tangerine orange and deep amber-gold, the colours shifting between them as she moves like something caught between fire and honey. Her wings are a softer clementine, and her underbelly is faint apricot. Waves of golden scales march down her spine and forearms, catching light like armour she was born wearing. Her claws are pure gold, and her eyes are amber with bright gold flecks in the iris — eyes that look like they are lit from behind.
Power
During moments of intense emotion — real joy, devastating grief, white-hot rage — Agate generates uncontrolled energy outbursts that command the wind. Early on, this power is entirely involuntary and frightening. She cannot predict it and cannot stop it. Later, as her character grows, she learns to reach for it deliberately — to turn a gust of emotion into a weapon, a shield, or a shelter for the people she loves. At her strongest, the winds around her respond to the direction of her thoughts before she even consciously commands them.
Personality Arc
She begins the story nervous and over-careful, someone who triple-checks everything and apologises out of reflex. Her cleverness is real but she hides it behind politeness, afraid of taking up too much space. Over the course of the series she becomes funny, warm, and startlingly calm under pressure — the kind of leader that others follow without being asked, and without her ever noticing it is happening. Her photographic memory is both her greatest tactical asset and her quiet burden — she never forgets anything. Not victories. Not wounds.
Role & Relationships
As the Queen's Regent, Agate holds a position of enormous political power and almost no personal freedom. She is bound to the throne's interests even when they conflict with her own. Her bond with Topaz runs deeper than she acknowledges out loud — they argue, they protect each other, they drive each other forward. Her relationship with Talon is quieter: a mutual respect between two people who understand what it means to carry something heavy without showing it. She is, by every measure, the emotional centre of the group.
T
Topaz
WindScale  ·  Prince  ·  Written by Daniel
WindScale Prince Snot Wind Bonded to Fang
Appearance
Topaz has the kind of presence that makes a room feel smaller. His scales are deep tangerine shot through with dark gold flecks, and his golden-brown eyes hold a sharp intelligence that evaluates everything and gives away nothing. He has a winding golden tail-band shaped as a coiled snake, a whip-thin tail, claws as sharp as obsidian, and teeth described as steel blades. A scar cuts through the thick of his tail — origin never officially recorded, though it came from a confrontation he refuses to explain. His pouch, worn at his side, has never been opened in front of anyone.
Power — The Snot Wind
Topaz can blast concentrated wind from his snout. This ability is officially, permanently, and non-negotiably named Snot Wind by Daniel, the author, under direct threat of resignation from TFS if changed. Despite the undignified name, it is genuinely devastating at close range — a focused burst of wind pressure from the snout that can knock a dragon twice his size off their feet, deflect projectiles mid-flight, and in combat, disorient an opponent's balance completely before Topaz follows with something worse.
Personality
Bitter, haughty, and protective in ways he would never voluntarily admit. Topaz has been let down before — by people he trusted, by the idea that royalty meant safety — and he has constructed an armour of arrogance around that history so effectively that most people never get close enough to see past it. The ones who do get a dragon who would walk into something catastrophic for them without hesitating. He has what the records describe as moon eyes for Agate — which he would deny, loudly, and unconvincingly.
The Bond with Fang
Topaz and Fang met when Topaz fled his home and stumbled upon the blue wolf during a pack hunt. They made eye contact and both felt something neither can properly explain — a recognition, a correspondence between their temperaments. They do not need language to communicate, though Fang can growl something approximating dragon words. Fang always knows where Topaz is. Topaz has never once asked him to leave.
T
Talon
ShardScale  ·  Servant of Ky  ·  Written by Raven
ShardScale Vegan No Power Bonded to Dyno
Appearance
Small and scrawny — easily overlooked, which suits him. Light icy-blue scales, with dark blue dots scattered across the underside of his wings like a constellation pressed into his skin. Electric-blue eyes that are sharper than they first appear. A small leather pouch hangs around his neck. He has never explained what is inside it, and no one has pushed hard enough to find out.
Power — None
In a world entirely defined by elemental power, Talon has nothing. No ability. No domain. No gift from his bloodline. He has survived entirely through wit, adaptability, and a stubbornness that refuses to let circumstances be the end of the story. Whether this makes him the weakest member of the group or the most dangerous depends entirely on who you ask. Several characters have made the mistake of assuming the former.
Personality
Deeply shy around everyone except the people he loves — with them, he is warm, quietly funny, and completely steady. Fiercely protective of Dyno, of Saiber, and most of all of Ky, who he regards not as a master but as a brother. He does not fight for glory. He fights because the alternative — losing one more person he loves — is not something he is willing to experience again. He is the kind of character who will never ask for help and will immediately drop everything to give it.
History
Just after hatching, Talon's parents and sister were taken from him by a palace guard. He was forced to pass a test to prove his worth before he was allowed to continue living in the palace. He saw his biological sister occasionally after — always by the Queen's side, always changed by it: snobby, self-entitled, unrecognisable. He stumbled into a friendship with Aquamarine through a collision and a mess of shattered glass, and she became the first person who felt like family. Then he found Ky. Then the others. Talon does not believe in luck. He believes in people.
A
Ash
MoonScale  ·  Royalty  ·  Written by Varsha
MoonScale Royalty Night-Powered Bonded to Storm
Appearance
Ash is the kind of dragon who could stand completely still in a dark room and become part of it. Her scales are black and deep navy-blue in a gradient that fades seamlessly into shadow — in dim light she is almost invisible, which is often deliberate. Tiny silver stars are scattered across her wings like actual sky pressed into membrane. Her eyes are blue, with a depth to them that seems greater than it should be, as though there is more light behind them than in front. In full moonlight, she is breathtaking. In daylight, she looks like something that belongs elsewhere.
Power
Ash draws power directly from the moon — which means her strength rises and falls with the lunar cycle. At a full moon, she is formidable. In direct sunlight, she is diminished, and she knows it. Her abilities fall into the moonlight category: subtle, vast, and deeply difficult to counter directly. She can perceive things others miss, especially in low light. She sees perfectly in absolute darkness. What else she can do is not yet fully recorded.
Personality
Guarded with strangers — not out of unkindness but out of earned caution. She has learned the hard way that trust given too quickly costs more than it saves. Get past that wall and you find someone who is fun, intensely driven, and frighteningly smart, with a dry humour that catches people off guard. She observes everything and comments on very little. The things she chooses to say are always worth listening to. She says nothing she does not mean.
Bond with Storm
Ash and Storm have a connection that neither of them has ever tried to explain, because neither of them thinks explanation would capture it. They simply understand each other — across distance, across silence, across moods neither of them voices. Storm's hunting instincts and Ash's strategic thinking have never once conflicted. They have never needed to argue about a plan, because they have never disagreed about what the right thing to do was.

Twilight
GalaxyScale  ·  "Twiy"  ·  Shared by all four authors
GalaxyScale Wanted Dual Domain Bonded to Angel
Appearance
Twilight's scales are pink and purple in a mix that shifts and catches light differently depending on the angle — stand in front of her and she looks one way, step to the side and she looks entirely different, like a gemstone that refuses to be singular. A dab of royal blue sits at the end of her tail. Her claws are rose gold. And her eyes — look at them closely and there is what appears to be a star-shaped marking in the iris, like a birthmark in the shape of a constellation. It does not move. It does not change. It has been there since she hatched.
Power
As a GalaxyScale, Twilight draws from the galaxy and stars simultaneously — light and dark in equal measure, neither one dominant. During moments of extreme emotion, she releases uncontrolled blasts of raw power and energy that are difficult to predict and harder to stop. She is one of the rarest and most potentially powerful types of dragon alive. Someone knows this. Someone has decided that this makes her a problem that needs to be resolved.
Personality
Kind without conditions, adventurous to a fault, and constitutionally incapable of being anything other than completely honest even when honesty is the least convenient possible option. She is funny and generous and clumsy in ways that make everyone around her simultaneously fond of her and slightly anxious. Her recklessness is not thoughtlessness — it is a deep, genuine belief that the world is worth running toward. She calls herself Twiy and most people who love her call her that too.
Status
Currently wanted by an unknown faction for reasons not yet disclosed in the official records. The nature of the threat and the identity of who issued it are not available. What is available: she does not know yet. And the people who do know have decided, for now, not to tell her.
The Books

Ten main volumes, three special stories, one quartet, and one special edition — each book building on the last, each one a step deeper into a world that keeps revealing new rooms. No spoilers. Only promises.

Book One
The Heartstonian Friendship
Where it begins. Five dragons from different tribes, different ranks, different worlds — thrown together by circumstances none of them chose and none of them expected. Before the powers, before the politics, before everything: the moment they became a group. Whether that means they are friends yet is a question the book leaves open. It is also the most important question in the series.
Book Two
A Revival
Something that was believed to be over is not over. Something that was believed to be dead is not dead. The group, barely held together after the events of Book One, must now contend with a threat that should not exist — and the discovery that some of what they were told about the world was deliberately, carefully wrong.
Book Three
The True Awakening
Powers begin to shift. Things that were dormant come online. Things that were assumed understood turn out to be only partly understood. For some members of the group, this is a gift. For others, it is the thing they were most afraid of. The world gets bigger and more dangerous in equal measure, and the five of them have to decide how far they are willing to go together.
Book Four
Heated Rebellion
Someone has had enough. A rebellion is building — not only among the dragons, but within the group itself. Loyalties that seemed certain are tested. Old alliances crack. Fire, in both the literal and metaphorical sense, is involved. This is the book where the series stops being about finding your place and starts being about fighting for it.
Book Five
The Fractured Crown
Royalty has never been safe, and now it is not even stable. The thrones of the dragon world are cracking — politically, physically, and personally. Ky's world begins to collapse. The question of who deserves power — and who has simply inherited it — becomes unavoidable.
Book Six
Shards of the Forgotten
History returns. Ancient Shard bloodlines that were believed extinct or erased begin to resurface — and with them, answers to questions the group has been asking since Book One. The world is older than anyone told them. The balance is older still. And it has been broken before.
Book Seven
When Stars Burn Dark
Twilight and Ash step into the foreground. The galaxy and the moon, light and dark, star and shadow — their arcs converge here in ways that neither of them anticipated. This is the book about what it means to carry power you did not ask for and what happens when you finally stop running from it.
Book Eight
The Last Correspondence
The Wheel is cracking. The Balance — the ancient system of opposing powers that keeps the world stable — is failing, and the group is at the centre of it. This is the book where the consequences of everything that has happened since Book One finally arrive all at once.
Book Nine
Eclipse of the Forbidden
Eclipse lives. Eclipse has always lived. And now Eclipse is no longer in the background. The most powerful dragon in the series steps forward — and the group must decide what side of that event they want to be on. The word "forbidden" finally becomes clear.
Book Ten
The Final Scale
The last book. The end of the main series. What was broken, what was built, what was lost, and what remains. After ten books of a world getting bigger and more dangerous, this is the one that asks: what does it look like when it finally settles? No spoilers. Only this: every main character gets an ending that is true to who they are.

The Shards

The most powerful dragon bloodlines in recorded history. Each Shard commands absolute dominion over a fundamental force of the world — not as a skill, not as a learned ability, but as a birthright written into their scales. Old scholars called them Shards because they believed each bloodline was a fragment broken from something far greater — a primordial power too vast for any single being to hold whole. No two Shards are equivalent. No Shard is safe to underestimate.

Flame
Domain of Fire & Heat
Core Power
A Flame Shard can transform their entire body into living fire — not a coating, not an aura, but a full physical conversion into something between dragon and star. The flames they produce burn at temperatures so extreme they can melt any known metal on contact, including dragon-forged alloys that take centuries to produce. Stone becomes liquid glass in sustained contact. Ice does not melt — it simply ceases to exist.
Extended Abilities
Flame Shards can command fire from any source within a wide radius — drawing existing flames toward them, feeding them, directing them as weapons or barriers. They are completely fireproof and cannot be burned by any temperature. Advanced Flame Shards can ignite the air itself, creating waves of burning pressure that knock enemies flat before the fire arrives. They leave scorched glass footprints when at full power. The most gifted can create sustained infernos that require no fuel — fire that burns without consuming anything, indefinitely.
Opposition
Countered most directly by the Sea Shard — enough water can suppress a Flame Shard's output, though a Flame Shard at full power fights back by evaporating water faster than it arrives. Wind can scatter their fire dangerously — a double-edged interaction that experienced Flame Shards learn to turn into a firestorm advantage. Their one true vulnerability is sustained cold: extreme enough cold can force them out of their transformed state entirely.
Sea
Domain of Water & Ice
Core Power
Sea Shards command water in every form — liquid, ice, vapour, and the transitional states between. They can pull moisture from the air itself, drawing it from clouds, from stone, from the exhalations of living things. Their senses are calibrated so precisely to water that they can detect any source within miles: underground rivers, rainfall before clouds form, hidden reservoirs beneath desert stone. What they find, they control. What they control, they can weaponise.
Extended Abilities
They can freeze water instantly — rivers into walls, rain into falling blades, waves into cages. Advanced Sea Shards can freeze the moisture in the air around a target, building a prison around them mid-motion without touching them. They breathe underwater indefinitely and are immune to pressure and cold extremes in water. Most unsettlingly: they can sense the water content of living bodies, and the most powerful among them have learned to use this knowledge in ways not documented in public-facing records.
Opposition
Countered by Flame Shards at peak power, who can evaporate water constructs faster than they can be rebuilt. Wind Shards can scatter wave-form attacks into useless mist. Crystal Shards can draw water into the earth, cutting off a Sea Shard's access to their medium. Their weakness is not heat alone — it is the combination of heat and separation from any water source.
Moon
Domain of Darkness & Perception
Core Power
Moon Shards do not destroy — they unmake the ability to perceive. They can reach into an enemy's visual field and darken their sight completely, as if ink were poured across the world — lasting from seconds to minutes depending on strength and the target's resistance. They can conjure spheres of pure, absolute darkness that expand like a held breath releasing, swallowing areas whole, inside which nothing can be seen and sound behaves strangely, bending away from its source.
Extended Abilities
Moon Shards are directly linked to the lunar cycle — at a full moon they are near-unstoppable, at a new moon significantly weakened. Beyond sight: they can muffle sound within their darkness zones, cutting communication between blinded opponents. Advanced Moon Shards can create a shadow-self — a duplicate made of darkness that moves independently, mirrors their actions, and confuses enemies in combat. The most powerful can create darkness that persists even after they leave an area, sustained by moonlight alone. They see perfectly in any darkness.
Opposition
Directly opposed by the Sun Shard — a single focused burst can shatter a Moon Shard's darkness sphere. Galaxy Shards cannot be countered by Moon power because they share it. Moon Shards operating in broad daylight are significantly weakened and tend to engage fast and close. Their deepest vulnerability is sustained, focused light — not ambient brightness, but directional, concentrated radiance.
Crystal
Domain of Earth & Armour
Core Power
Crystal Shards can manifest armour from nothing — pulling mineral matter from the earth, from stone, from minerals dissolved in the air itself, forming it into perfectly-structured crystalline plates around themselves or an ally in seconds. This armour is not simple rock: it is harder than anything produced in any forge, structured at a molecular level by the Shard's power into a lattice that distributes impact instead of absorbing it. They can also command the earth — shift stone, raise walls, crack ground open with precision.
Extended Abilities
Advanced Crystal Shards can extend their armour outward into spikes, shields, blades, and barriers — wearing the battlefield as an extension of their body. Even if their armour shatters, they simply pull more from the ground beneath them. They can sense structural weaknesses in anything solid — walls, terrain, armour on other dragons — and know precisely where to strike for maximum effect. The most powerful have been recorded encasing opponents in crystal entirely, freezing them mid-motion in a transparent prison they cannot break from the inside.
Opposition
Their armour has limits under sustained Flame Shard heat — crystal cracks and eventually melts. Sea Shards can undermine the ground beneath them, removing their footing and material supply. Wind Shards find their armour difficult to penetrate but can topple them with brute force — a Crystal Shard on its back cannot dig up new material quickly. Their weakness is isolation from the earth: mid-air, they lose access to most of what makes them devastating.
Wind
Domain of Air & Motion
Core Power
Wind Shards bend the air entirely to their will — from a current gentle enough to carry a whisper miles away to a sustained gale that levels forests and strips rock faces bare. The wind is not only a weapon: it is a sixth sense. They feel every disturbance in the air around them — movement, approaching bodies, even emotional states read through the subtle shifts in what a living creature exhales. They are nearly impossible to ambush. They know you are coming before you decide to come.
Extended Abilities
Wind Shards can fly at speeds that make them nearly impossible to track visually. They can create localised vacuums — pockets with no air — which is as effective against fire as water, and suffocating against anything that breathes. They can redirect projectiles mid-flight, deflect elemental attacks, and strip the air from enclosed spaces. Elite Wind Shards create contained storms: vortexes that trap opponents, twisters that scour terrain, walls of wind that deflect everything including sound. They can also listen across enormous distances by riding their currents as a kind of long-range hearing.
Opposition
Crystal Shards can anchor themselves against wind, making them immovable. Sea Shards can flood terrain, grounding a Wind Shard's aerial advantage. Flame Shards can redirect their gusts as superheated firestorms — the most dangerous interaction in the Wheel, where Wind's strength becomes the weapon used against it. Their weakness is enclosure: in a sealed space, their advantage shrinks dramatically.
Sun
Domain of Light & Radiance
Core Power
Sun Shards can unleash blinding flashes of concentrated light — not ordinary brightness but focused photonic force that overwhelms visual processing entirely, leaving enemies sightless for extended periods. They can also become a living beacon, radiating brilliance visible from impossible distances. Their light is not decorative — it is directional, concentrated, and capable of revealing anything hidden in shadow, underground, or obscured by Moon Shard power.
Extended Abilities
Sun Shards draw directly from sunlight — dramatically stronger at midday and in open terrain. They can focus their radiance into a narrow beam of concentrated solar energy capable of cutting through stone. Advanced Sun Shards maintain light constructs: sustained walls of radiance that hurt to approach, illuminated decoys that distract in combat, and persistent beacons that operate independently. Some scholars believe powerful Sun Shards can accelerate biological processes using focused light — healing, growth, and in theory, the reverse. They are always visible. Their power announces their location. This is the price they pay for it.
Opposition
Directly opposed by the Moon Shard — darkness spheres absorb and nullify Sun light. At night without artificial sources, a Sun Shard is severely weakened. Dense storm clouds — producible by Sea or Wind Shards — cut off their solar power mid-battle. They cannot use their power subtly: light always announces them. Against an enemy who knows they are coming, their most powerful ability becomes their most predictable one.
Galaxy
The Dual Shard  ·  Domain of All Light & All Dark
Full Moon Powers Full Sun Powers Dual Domain Extremely Rare Hunted
What Makes Them Different
Galaxy Shards do not split two powers between them. They carry the full power of the Moon and the full power of the Sun simultaneously, held in the same body without cancelling each other out. Old scholars believed this to be impossible — that light and dark in equal measure would neutralise into nothing. Instead they compound. A Galaxy Shard can flood a battlefield with blinding radiance and snuff every light source in existence in the same breath. They are not a compromise. They are both, entire.
The Unique Galaxy Ability — Spatial Perception
Because Galaxy Shards command both what can be seen and what cannot, they develop something that belongs to neither parent domain alone: spatial perception. An awareness of the space between light and dark — the shapes of shadow, the angles at which light bends. In practice: they can detect cloaked or invisible things that even Moon Shards miss. They can track the path of light bending around obstacles. Advanced Galaxy Shards can bend light around their own form, rendering themselves invisible — not in darkness, but in plain sight, which is the more unsettling version. The most extreme cases in the historical record involve Galaxy Shards generating both a blinding flash and a total darkness field simultaneously, creating a zone of complete sensory disorientation — enemies see nothing, then too much, then nothing again, with no warning between transitions.
Why They Are Hunted
No other single Shard type can fully counter a Galaxy Shard. Moon Shards cannot use darkness against them — they share it, and the Galaxy carries it stronger. Sun Shards cannot blind them — they invented the ability. The only meaningful weaknesses are those of either parent domain in isolation, and a skilled Galaxy Shard rotates between powers fast enough that neither weakness is ever consistently applicable. This is not a power that should exist in one dragon. The scholars who wrote about it were aware of this. So, apparently, are certain parties currently unknown to the general population — which is why Twilight, the only confirmed living Galaxy Shard in the current records, is wanted.
The Great Balance

Nothing in the world of the Forbidden Scales exists without its opposite. The ancient scholars called it the Correspondence — the principle that the world maintains itself not through peace, but through opposition. Every power has a mirror. Every strength has a counter. When one Shard rises unchecked, another is drawn toward it like tide to shore. This is not coincidence. It is a law as old as the bloodlines themselves. And like all laws, it can be broken.

Flame
opposes
Sea
The oldest opposition. Flame burns hot enough to unmake metal — but Sea does not simply douse it. A Sea Shard can freeze the moisture in the air around a Flame Shard before the fire can reach full temperature, encasing them in ice during the window of vulnerability before they complete their transformation. Flame Shards at peak power evaporate water faster than it arrives — but Sea Shards draw from the atmosphere itself, from underground rivers, from clouds miles distant. The contest is not about which runs hotter or colder. It is about which runs out first — and which fighter has the patience to make that the question.

The compound interaction: Flame and Wind together are catastrophic — fire fed by air becomes a spreading inferno that a Wind Shard can direct at will. Sea and Crystal reinforce each other — water directed into earth channels, earth holding water in formations a Sea Shard could not maintain alone.
"The sea does not fear the fire. The sea simply remembers that fire needs air — and the sea has drowned things before they could ever burn." — Scholar's marginalia, volume and author unknown.
Sun
opposes
Moon
The most personal opposition. Sun and Moon Shards do not simply cancel each other's abilities — they unravel each other's entire purpose. A Moon Shard's darkness sphere, which can swallow a battlefield and leave armies helpless, is shattered instantly by a single focused Sun burst. A Sun Shard's blinding flash — terrifying against every other type — washes harmlessly over a Moon Shard who sees perfectly in absolute dark.

What makes this opposition extraordinary is that neither is dominant at all times: in full daylight the Moon Shard weakens; at night the Sun Shard dims. They are calibrated against the world's own rhythm — day and night, the cycle that predates every dragon bloodline. They were not made to destroy each other. They were made to balance each other, which is why the Galaxy Shard — who carries both — is not a balance. It is what happens when the system that requires a balance no longer functions. A Galaxy Shard is not a mediator. It is a correction.
"Light makes darkness visible. Darkness makes light meaningful. Take either away entirely and you do not get more of the other — you get blindness of a different kind." — Recovered text, author unknown, estimated to predate the current tribal system by several centuries.
Wind
opposes
Crystal
The opposition between the untethered and the immovable. Wind is formless, invisible, and omnipresent — it cannot be held, caught, or permanently stopped. Crystal is the inverse: dense, permanent, built to endure. A Wind Shard can crack a Crystal Shard's armour with enough sustained directional force — stone fractures under pressure if you know the angle, and Crystal Shards can sense structural weaknesses in everything except air itself. A Crystal Shard can drive itself into the earth and become immovable, removing the Wind Shard's primary tactic of throwing things around.

The tension is mobility versus durability. Wind Shards are the fastest combatants in the world. Crystal Shards are the most protected. In a sustained engagement, Crystal wins — attrition favours armour. In a fast engagement, Wind wins — speed favours evasion. This is why Wind Shards never, ever let a fight become a sustained engagement. And why Crystal Shards always try to make it one.
"The mountain does not chase the wind. It does not need to. Every storm passes. The mountain is still there." — Traditional Crystal Shard proverb, used in combat training for several generations.
The Correspondence — The Full Wheel
The six base Shards form what the old scholars called the Wheel of Correspondence. No point on the Wheel has no counter. No two adjacent powers exist without tension between them. The oppositions are direct: Flame and Sea. Sun and Moon. Wind and Crystal. But the Wheel also turns across adjacencies: Flame and Wind compound into inferno. Sea and Crystal reinforce into fortified terrain. Sun and Flame share warmth — neither harms the other, together they are relentless. Moon and Sea share the tide — Sea Shards near Moon Shards report expanded range and heightened sensitivity to water they cannot directly see.
The Galaxy Exception
The Galaxy Shard sits entirely outside the Wheel. Early scholars placed it at the centre — holding both light and dark, it seemed like the keeper of balance, the mediator. The more recent and more uncomfortable theory: the Galaxy Shard is not the centre of the Wheel. It is what appears when the Wheel stops turning. Every several centuries, when one Shard bloodline grows too dominant, or another is brought to the edge of extinction, or the opposing forces tip so far that the system cannot self-correct — a Galaxy Shard is born. Not as a ruler. Not as a hero. As a correction mechanism. Whether this is reassuring or terrifying depends entirely on which side of the imbalance you are standing on when it arrives.

The Wolf Pack

The Red Wolf pack — 28 members strong, led by Fang and the injured Storm. Bonded to the dragon companions through something older than language. Not pets. Not weapons. Not servants. Partners — with their own hierarchy, their own opinions, and their own reasons for being here.

Fang — Alpha Male
Coat: Blue. Eyes: Dark gold with orange-red flecks. Claws: Silver. Diet: Vegan — loves berries, sugarcane, and vegetables. Sugarcane in particular.

Abilities: Can run at the speed of sound for 5,000 miles before dropping to Topaz's flight speed. Can sense Topaz's exact location at any distance. Can detect any living animal within a 10-mile radius. Can growl words approximating Dragon language. Can appear to teleport when moving laterally at top speed. Has photographic memory. Can call his entire pack from anywhere on the continent with a single howl.

Bond: Fang and Topaz met when Topaz was fleeing his home and stumbled into a pack hunting formation. They made eye contact in a moment neither of them has ever adequately explained. They simply stayed. They have not needed a different reason since. Fang is the Alpha of the Red Wolves and takes that role as seriously as any position in the group — his leadership of the pack is absolute, earned, and respected. His loyalty to Topaz operates on a different frequency than loyalty to the pack, which is saying something, because his loyalty to the pack would walk into anything.
Freki
Main Healer
Breed: Eastern & Red. Diet: Pescatarian.
Appearance: Dusky blue and silver coat, white underbelly, dusky red at ear tips, tail tip, and legs. Black ear-insides.

Deeply loyal to Storm above all others. She loves collecting herbs, learning about plants, caring for pups in her free time, rolling in snow, and swimming. Can run 35–40 mph for 450 miles. Her sense of smell, sight, and hearing are all exceptional — calibrated by years of herb-finding to distinguish things others miss entirely. Photographic memory specifically for plant identification. Can speak Dragon vaguely, though she prefers not to. She will fight for her pack if she must, and she will win.
Echo
Head of Defense
Breed: Red. Diet: Omnivorous.
Appearance: Silky red fur, black splotches, strong muscles, deep piercing black eyes, sturdy claws.

Strong and deeply confident — many respect Echo, others fear her. Both reactions are appropriate. She has a loud howl and exceptional stealth, which is a combination that should not work but does. Firmly loyal to Fang and Storm, with a slight lean toward Fang. She is not unfriendly to strangers — she is simply unwilling to extend trust until it is earned, which is a different thing. She will follow orders without question from the Alphas. Everyone else needs to demonstrate their worth first.
Chair
Scout Leader
Breed: Red. Diet: Pescatarian.
Appearance: Whitish-red fur, maroon splotches.

Chair is serious to a professional fault. He dislikes pups — not in an abstract way but with a specific, sustained, documented reluctance to be near them. He is bossy, prone to snapping at subordinates, and trusts his scout team while giving very little indication of this. In his free time he sits around lazily — which is, in direct fact, the origin of his name. Despite all of this, his scouting unit has never failed to return with accurate information. He is very good at his job. He just makes sure nobody enjoys working with him.
Bolt
Gatherer Leader
Breed: Red. Diet: Meat.
Appearance: Red-tinted green fur, brownish-maroon underbelly, leaf-shaped brown markings across his back.

Extremely fast and resourceful. Prefers to be alone, does not consider this a problem, and will tell you so if asked. Has anger issues that are triggered specifically by loud noises and sudden movements — he lashes out without full control when either occurs. Being around Hail almost completely manages this, a fact he has never commented on to her directly. Has a soft spot for Hail that he does not acknowledge. Wants Hail as his mate. Has not said this to anyone.
Aspen
Hunter Leader
Breed: Red. Diet: Pescatarian.
Appearance: Bright red fur, black underbelly, heterochromia — brown left eye, yellow right eye. Strong muscles.

Kind and results-oriented simultaneously, which is rarer than it sounds. She hates procrastination with a specificity that suggests a formative experience. Gets along with healers and caretakers, prefers their company over guards and scouts. Likes Kodiak. Has not said this to anyone either. In her free time she carves small wooden figures of wolves — every member of the pack, in miniature, and a few she keeps private.
Hail
Caretaker Leader
Breed: Arctic & Red. Diet: Pescatarian.
Appearance: Light blue fur with red flecks, deep soft golden-brown eyes, silver-tipped tail, retractable teal claws.

Protective, kind, and extraordinarily intelligent — she can solve any logistical problem the pack faces and has done so repeatedly. Fang has told her she should be a scout. She has declined this, consistently, because she prefers the pups. She spends more time than required with all of them but most with Taco, the only pup in the pack that nobody else can manage. Taco listens to Hail. Nobody has explained this. It simply is.
Storm — Female Alpha (Injured)
Breed: Arctic & Red. Appearance: White with specks of blue, red features, red eyes. Diet: Meat.

Abilities: Top speed 250 mph — currently reduced to 40 mph for 250 miles due to a paw injury sustained in an incident whose details are not in the public record. Whether she will recover full speed is unclear. She is an excellent hunter regardless and always returns to Ash. Ash and Storm understand each other without language — a bond that has never needed explaining and has never received one.

Mate: Fang. Their dynamic is the axis around which the pack's emotional structure rotates. When Storm was injured, the pack tightened. When Storm is present, the pack breathes easier. She is, by general pack consensus, the reason Fang is the Alpha he is rather than just a very fast wolf with an attitude.
Companions & Familiars

Not every bond in this series is between dragons. Some of the most important relationships are between a dragon and the smaller, stranger creature that decided — without being asked — that this particular dragon was worth staying near.

Saiber
Elina's companion  ·  Male peregrine falcon
Grayish-blue head, brown and white wings. Black eyes with shadowed golden irises — the kind of gaze that makes it feel like he has already evaluated you and made a quiet decision. Loves strawberries and marshmallows specifically, and has opinions about the quality of both. Non-mythical, which in this world means he has no power beyond being a peregrine falcon — which is, objectively, enough. The fastest dive of any bird on earth. He does not need to be magical. He is already extraordinary.
Dyno
Raven's companion  ·  Male hawk
A hawk with an enormous personality relative to his physical size. Loves blueberries. Is, by all documented evidence across the entire records, a narcissist — this is not presented as a character flaw in the story so much as a perspective. He is very interested in himself and somewhat interested in events insofar as they involve him. His chapter, Dyno's Story, is narrated entirely by him, and the reliability of that narration is left as an exercise for the reader.
Storm
Varsha's companion  ·  Female Alpha wolf
Arctic-Red hybrid. White coat with blue specks and red features. Red eyes. Currently injured in one paw — top speed reduced from 250 mph to 40 mph during recovery. Agile, an exceptional hunter, fiercely loyal, and always returns to Ash. Mate of Fang, Female Alpha of the Red Wolf pack. She and Ash understand each other completely. The nature of that understanding is not something either of them has tried to put into words.

The Laws of the World
No omnipotent characters — Eclipse is one standing exception, along with the shards, and they all still has consequences.
No all-powerful magic — The power to wish for anything or will for anything to either come into creation or happen is prohibited by the world's rules, not just the authors'.
No mythicals except dragons — the wolves are extraordinary but not mythical.
Body limits apply — every dragon must fit within their Shard type's established physical range.
Four pages minimum per chapter — no exceptions have been granted.
Companions stay companions — they are part of the story, not the main characters.
The Current Font
The Forbidden Scales manuscript is currently typeset in Times New Roman — chosen for its cleen, standard book quality. Though for some parts of the book, the font may change depending on the context. (Like a diary made by Ky)
